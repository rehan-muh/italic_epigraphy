The map shows the posterior mean of a shared latent field E(s,t) fitted jointly to dated cities, Pleiades settlements and infrastructure, shipwrecks, amphora stamps, ports, Republican coin hoards, presses, mines and radiocarbon dates on a 0.25° × 25-year grid. Values are in standard deviations of the field over the whole space–time window, so blue is below the 700–1 BCE mean and red above it; the field rises through the period everywhere, and the interesting part is where it rises first. Play through time, scrub to a year, hover a cell, or pin a place to follow its trajectory with its 95% interval.

How to read it

The field is a latent-factor log-Gaussian Cox process: each evidence source has its own intensity exp(αk + ρkE + δd), with the loading ρk estimated rather than chosen and a residual δd for what a domain (settlement, exchange, connectivity, production, monetization) shows beyond the shared signal. The length scales (140 km, 50 yr) were selected by spatial-block cross-validation; chronological uncertainty enters as aoristic probability over the 25-year bins, never as midpoint dates. Low values in poorly surveyed regions are partly a survey effect: switch the layer to the posterior SD before reading a contrast, and prefer the domain fields for hypotheses about trade or settlement specifically.

Model card, source ledger and fitted loadings: data/economy/field_v04/README.md in the repository. Evidence: Hanson (OXREP cities), Pleiades (CC BY), OXREP shipwrecks, mines and presses, CEIPAC amphora stamps (Rubio et al. 2018), de Graauw's ancient ports, Coin Hoards of the Roman Republic (CC BY-NC), AIDA radiocarbon.
